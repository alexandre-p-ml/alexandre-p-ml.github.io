Medical Image Segmentation

Challenge ENS Data - Raidium

Published

January 1, 2026

Lien GitHub

📓 Notebook

Overview

ENS Raidium challenge on semantic segmentation of abdominal CT slices (256×256), with 55 classes (background + 54 anatomical organs and structures).

  • 2,000 training images, of which only 800 were annotated while 1,200 were completely unlabeled.
  • Partial annotations: for each image, only a subset of classes was guaranteed to be labeled. The remaining classes were neither positive nor negative and therefore had to be ignored during loss computation.
  • Approximately 90% of pixels belong to the background, while several classes appear in ten images or fewer.
  • Evaluation on 500 test images using macro Dice score on the competition platform.

Approach & Results

Stage Method Leaderboard score
Baseline U-Net ResNet-18, cross entropy 0,022
Loss masquée + Composite Dice + class weighting 0,169
Consolidation + Data augmentation + corrected Dice + 60 epochs 0,270
Semi-supervisé + Mean Teacher (1,200 unlabeled images) 0,329
Latent + Supervised Contrastive Loss (SupCon) 0,385
Inférence + Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) 0,4005
Ciblage + Class-specific weighting 0,4023

I progressively improved a ResNet-18 U-Net by first adapting the loss function to the partial annotation protocol, computing the loss only over certified classes, which increased the leaderboard Dice from 0.022 to 0.169. I then leveraged the 1,200 unlabeled images using a Mean Teacher semi-supervised framework, reaching 0.329.

To further improve rare-class discrimination, I analyzed the latent space using PCA and introduced Supervised Contrastive Learning (SupCon), which raised the score to 0.385 despite a decrease in the latent-space silhouette score. Finally, Test-Time Augmentation provided a nearly free improvement, bringing the public leaderboard score to 0.4005.

The remaining performance gap was ultimately explained by the evaluation metric: the competition used an unmasked macro Dice, where every anatomical class contributes equally regardless of its frequency. As a result, missing a few extremely rare classes had a disproportionately large impact on the final score, whereas my local masked validation substantially overestimated leaderboard performance.

The next planned step was to adopt nnU-Net v2, the current reference framework for medical image segmentation and the foundation of the upcoming DinoUNet architecture based on DINOv3.

Per-Class Statistical Analysis

  • Mean Dice per class: approximately 0.43 (median 0.47, standard deviation 0.28), closely matching the leaderboard score and confirming the macro-average evaluation.
  • Six classes achieved exactly zero Dice (classes 1, 2, 15, 25, 26 and 40), while nine additional classes remained below 0.10. Together, these fifteen classes reduced the macro-average by roughly 0.15–0.20.
  • Eleven classes achieved Dice ≥ 0.70, with the best class reaching 0.96.
  • Pearson correlation between anatomical structure size and Dice was approximately 0.64. The five largest structures achieved an average Dice of 0.85, compared with only 0.11 for the five smallest.

Negative Results / Fails

Frozen DINOv2 (ViT-S/14)

Using a self-supervised Vision Transformer pretrained on 142 million images appeared promising but validation performance stayed around 0.40, far below the 0.67 obtained by the ResNet-18 pipeline on the same split.

Three factors likely explain this result:

  • Significant domain shift: DINOv2 was pretrained on natural RGB photographs whereas abdominal CT consists of grayscale medical scans.
  • Frozen backbone: the 21M-parameter encoder remained fixed, preventing the decoder from adapting representations to the target domain.
  • Coarse 18×18 patch representation poorly suited for thin anatomical boundaries and lacking the strong local inductive bias of convolutional networks.

nnU-Net

Experiments were initiated on an RTX 3050 (6 GB VRAM). Dataset conversion, manifest generation, and custom fold definition all completed successfully. However nnU-Net automatically configured a memory budget that resulted in training times close to one hour per epoch making full experimentation impractical on the available hardware. DinoUNet relies on the same infrastructure.

Lessons Learned

The main remaining limitation was the extremely rare classes. Even aggressive class weighting could not recover structures appearing in only a handful of training images.

Reweighting classes inversely proportional to their Dice score improved 9 out of 12 targeted classes (one increased from 0.30 to 0.56), yet the overall leaderboard improvement remained marginal (+0.0018). Under a macro Dice over 54 classes each class contributes only about 1.85% of the final score and no loss function can compensate for the absence of meaningful training signal when only a few annotated examples are available.
